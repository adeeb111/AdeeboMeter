== path.PC
friendly: Your PC
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Step one: is the problem on this laptop?
what: Checks whether the laptop itself is the bottleneck - CPU, memory and threads.
how: Reads Windows' own counters once a second. No network involved.
why: If the PC is choking, every website feels slow - and that's not the network's fault.
without: Red here means close something heavy. The other dots can be green and it still feels slow.

== path.Wi-Fi|path.LAN
friendly: Your local link
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Step two: the hop from you to your router.
what: Shows how well you reach your router (the gateway) - over Wi-Fi or a cable.
how: Pings the gateway every few seconds and reads Wi-Fi signal, channel and speed from Windows.
why: This is where most home and hotel problems live: weak signal, crowded channel, a busy router.
without: Slow or lost gateway pings mean the trouble is in the room - move closer, plug in a cable, or restart the router.

== path.ISP|path.VPN
friendly: Your provider / VPN
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Step three: from your router out to the world.
what: Shows the jump from your router into your internet provider - or into the company VPN.
how: Compares the time to the internet with the time to your gateway; the difference is what the provider (or VPN) adds.
why: When this step is slow, rebooting your laptop won't help.
without: A big jump past the router means the provider or the VPN gateway is slow - not you.

== path.DNS
friendly: DNS
cat: term
close: -1
tag: The internet's phone book.
what: Turns names like github.com into addresses computers can dial.
how: Times a real lookup to the DNS server Windows is using, skipping the cache.
why: If DNS is slow, every new website waits before it even starts loading.
without: Slow or failing DNS feels like 'the internet is broken' even when the link is fine. On VPN, DNS goes through the tunnel.

== path.Internet
friendly: The internet
cat: term
close: -1
tag: The last step: can you reach the outside world?
what: Checks that a well-known public address answers.
how: One small ping to a public DNS server (1.1.1.1 or 8.8.8.8) every 30 seconds - more often while this window is open.
why: Tells 'my Wi-Fi is fine' apart from 'the internet is down'.
without: No reply with a healthy gateway means the outage is past your router. Some networks block ping - then this stays grey.

== TCP retransmits
friendly: TCP retransmits
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Packets that had to be sent twice.
what: Counts TCP segments Windows had to send again because no acknowledgement came back.
how: Read from Windows' TCP statistics every second - the same numbers 'netstat -s' shows.
why: A few is normal. Lots means packets are being lost somewhere - weak Wi-Fi, a congested link, a bad cable.
without: Steady retransmits make downloads crawl and calls stutter. Check the 'Why:' line - it says when they started.

== TCP resets sent
friendly: TCP resets sent
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Connections slammed shut.
what: Counts TCP resets (RST) - a hard 'go away' instead of a polite goodbye.
how: Read from Windows' TCP statistics.
why: Bursts happen when apps close or a firewall cuts sessions.
without: A sudden flood means something is killing connections - a VPN reconnect, a firewall, or an app crashing.

== Failed connection attempts
friendly: Failed connection attempts
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Knocks on doors that never opened.
what: Counts connections that never got established.
how: Read from Windows' TCP statistics, totalled over the last minute.
why: Shows when apps are trying to reach things they can't.
without: Many failures mean a blocked port, a dead server, a proxy problem or a VPN that dropped.

== ICMP unreachable received
friendly: ICMP unreachable
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Routers saying 'can't get there from here'.
what: Counts 'destination unreachable' messages routers and hosts send back.
how: Read from Windows' ICMP statistics.
why: Tells you a route or a port is closed somewhere along the way.
without: A stream of these usually means a routing problem or a firewall rejecting traffic.

== UDP datagrams dropped
friendly: UDP datagrams dropped
cat: term
close: -1
tag: UDP packets that arrived with nowhere to go.
what: Counts UDP packets Windows had to throw away - no app listening, or a full buffer.
how: Read from Windows' UDP statistics.
why: UDP carries DNS, Teams media and VPN (DTLS) traffic.
without: Lots of drops during a call can mean the PC is too busy to keep up.

== CPU
friendly: CPU
cat: term
close: -1
tag: How hard the processor is working.
what: Share of processor time in use across all cores.
how: Read from Windows (GetSystemTimes) every second.
why: When it's pegged, everything waits in line - typing, video, calls.
without: Over 85% for long: look at 'Top by CPU' and close the hungriest app.

== Memory
friendly: Memory
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Your desk space.
what: How much of your RAM is in use. 'Committed' is everything apps have asked for, including what spilled to the page file.
how: Read from Windows (GlobalMemoryStatusEx).
why: When memory runs out, Windows shuffles to disk and everything slows to a crawl.
without: Above 90%: close a big app - 'Top by memory' shows who.

== Disk
friendly: Disk
cat: term
close: -1
tag: How busy the drive is.
what: Share of time the disk is busy reading or writing.
how: Windows' PhysicalDisk performance counters.
why: A busy disk makes apps freeze for a moment when they open files.
without: Pinned at 100% means updates, antivirus scans, OneDrive or Outlook syncs - usually it passes.

== Download
friendly: Download
cat: term
close: -1
tag: How much is flowing in right now.
what: Megabits per second arriving on your active network adapter.
how: Windows' adapter byte counters, read every second.
why: Tells you whether a slow site is slow - or your line is simply full.
without: Near your line's limit: something is downloading (updates, OneDrive) and everything else waits.

== Top by memory
friendly: Top by memory
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Who's using your desk space.
what: The apps holding the most RAM, with all their processes added up.
how: Process list read every 10 seconds (every 2 s with this window open).
why: The quickest way to know what to close.
without: Browsers and Teams add up fast - each tab or panel is its own process.

== Top by CPU
friendly: Top by CPU
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Who's keeping the processor busy.
what: The apps using the most processor time right now, all their processes added up.
how: Process CPU time compared between two reads.
why: Finds the culprit when the fan spins up.
without: Antivirus and indexing spikes come and go; an app stuck high is the one to close.

== step.DNS
friendly: DNS lookup
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Find the address.
what: Asks your DNS server for the name's address (IPv4 and IPv6), skipping the cache.
how: A real query through Windows' DNS client, timed.
why: No address, no connection - everything else depends on this.
without: 'No such name' = typo or an internal name that needs the VPN. 'Timed out' = DNS server trouble.

== step.Route
friendly: Route
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Which door it leaves by.
what: Shows which adapter Windows would use to reach that address - Wi-Fi, cable or VPN.
how: Asks Windows' routing table by opening a UDP socket - nothing is sent.
why: Catches 'it's going around the VPN' (or through it) surprises.
without: 'No route' means Windows has no path at all - adapter down or VPN routes missing.

== step.Ping
friendly: Ping
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Is anyone home?
what: Four ICMP echo requests to the address.
how: Measures the round trip and how many replies came back.
why: A quick health check of the path.
without: Many servers block ping on purpose - 'no reply' alone doesn't mean it's down.

== step.TCP
friendly: TCP connect
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Knock on the door.
what: Opens a TCP connection to the port.
how: The three-way handshake: SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK - timed.
why: If TCP connects, the network path and the firewall are fine.
without: 'Refused' = server is there but nothing listens on that port. 'Timeout' = a firewall is silently dropping, or the server is down.

== step.TLS
friendly: TLS handshake
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Agree on a secret and check ID.
what: Sets up encryption and checks the server's certificate with Windows.
how: TLS 1.2 or 1.3 only. Windows validates the certificate chain; if it isn't trusted, AdeeboMeter stops and sends nothing.
why: This is where inspection proxies, expired certificates and MTU black holes show up.
without: 'Untrusted' = certificate problem or a proxy in the middle. 'Stalls' = something drops the big certificate packets (often MTU).

== step.HTTP
friendly: HTTP
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Does the website answer?
what: Sends one HEAD request - like asking 'are you there?' without downloading the page.
how: Reads only the status line (200 OK, 404, 503...) and the time to first byte.
why: Separates 'the network is fine' from 'the site itself is broken'.
without: 4xx = you're not allowed or the page is gone. 5xx = the server is broken - not you.

== step.Path MTU
friendly: Path MTU
cat: term
close: -1
tag: The biggest packet that fits through the pipe.
what: Finds the largest packet that reaches the server without being split.
how: Sends pings with 'don't fragment' set and narrows the size down.
why: Tunnels (VPN, LTE, Starlink) shrink the pipe. Big packets that don't fit vanish.
without: Below 1500 is normal on VPN. Trouble starts when big packets disappear silently - pages hang half-loaded.

== step.Path
friendly: Path (traceroute)
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Every router on the way.
what: Lists the routers between you and the server, with time and loss at each.
how: MTR-style: three rounds of probes with growing hop limits, all at once.
why: Shows where along the way the delay or loss begins.
without: Loss on a middle hop that doesn't continue to the end is just a router ignoring pings - only loss that carries on to the last hop matters.

== card.SIGN-IN PAGE (CAPTIVE PORTAL)
friendly: Sign-in page check
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Hotel and airport Wi-Fi, caught red-handed.
what: Asks the same tiny test page Windows uses to decide 'sign-in required'.
how: One plain HTTP request to msftconnecttest.com; if anything other than Microsoft's answer comes back, a portal is in the way. The redirect is never followed.
why: A sign-in page blocks everything until you accept it - and makes every other test look broken.
without: 'Found' = open a browser and sign in, then check again.

== card.WHO'S TALKING TO IT
friendly: Who's talking to it
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Apps on this PC connected to that destination.
what: Lists the apps on your PC with open connections to that address right now.
how: Read from Windows' connection table - no capture needed.
why: Answers 'which app is it?' in one glance.
without: Nobody = no app is connected to it at the moment.

== state.ESTABLISHED
friendly: ESTABLISHED
cat: term
close: -1
tag: A live conversation.
what: Both sides are connected and can send data.
how: The TCP handshake finished.
why: This is what working connections look like.
without: Lots of them to one place is normal for browsers and Teams.

== state.SYN_SENT
friendly: SYN_SENT
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Knocked - waiting for an answer.
what: Your PC asked to connect and is waiting for the reply.
how: The first packet of the TCP handshake went out; no SYN-ACK yet.
why: A few for a split second is normal.
without: Stuck for seconds = blocked by a firewall, a dead server, or a route that goes nowhere.

== state.TIME_WAIT
friendly: TIME_WAIT
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Hung up - waiting a moment to be sure.
what: The connection closed; Windows keeps the slot for a minute or two.
how: So late packets from the old conversation can't confuse a new one. Windows shows these as owned by process 0.
why: Normal after browsing.
without: Thousands at once means an app is opening and closing connections very fast.

== state.CLOSE_WAIT
friendly: CLOSE_WAIT
cat: term
close: -1
tag: The other side hung up; this app hasn't.
what: The server closed the connection and the app on your PC hasn't closed its end yet.
how: Waiting for the app to call close().
why: Usually brief.
without: Many piling up = an app isn't cleaning up after itself (a bug).

== state.LISTEN|state.BOUND
friendly: LISTEN / BOUND
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Waiting for calls, or holding a number.
what: LISTEN = an app waits for incoming connections. BOUND = a port reserved but not in use.
how: Shown only when you turn on 'Bound / listening'.
why: Tells you which apps accept connections into your PC.
without: AdeeboMeter itself never listens - it checks that about itself every time it starts.

== state.SYN_RCVD
friendly: SYN_RCVD
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Someone knocked on your door.
what: Another machine asked to connect to an app on your PC, and your PC answered.
how: The middle step of the TCP handshake, seen only on ports an app listens on.
why: Rare on a laptop.
without: Lots of them = something is trying to connect in (a scan, or a misconfigured app).

== state.CLOSED|state.DELETE_TCB
friendly: CLOSED
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Finished - about to disappear.
what: The connection is fully closed and Windows is tidying up.
how: The last step after a goodbye or a reset; the row vanishes moments later.
why: Normal and short-lived.
without: Nothing to do.

== state.FIN_WAIT1|state.FIN_WAIT2|state.LAST_ACK|state.CLOSING
friendly: Closing states
cat: term
close: -1
tag: Saying goodbye.
what: The connection is in the middle of closing.
how: TCP closes in a polite four-step goodbye; these are the steps.
why: Normal and short-lived.
without: Lots stuck here = a firewall dropping the goodbye packets.

== live.CALL QUALITY|live.MOS
friendly: Call quality
cat: term
close: -1
tag: How your calls really sound - measured, not guessed.
what: Loss, jitter and an estimated MOS score (1-4.5) for each call media stream (Teams and other RTP calls).
how: Sequence numbers show lost packets; timestamps vs. arrival show jitter (RFC 3550); the E-model (ITU-T G.107) turns them, plus your internet delay, into a MOS. Encrypted audio stays encrypted - only headers are read.
why: MOS above 4 is great, 3.6-4 is fine, below 3.6 people start to notice.
without: High loss = network trouble on the way; high jitter = a busy link or Wi-Fi. Fix the first red thing on the path.
