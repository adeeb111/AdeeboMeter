Internet slow every evening? Keep the comparison fair.

An evening pattern is useful evidence. It can reflect activity at home, Wi-Fi conditions or congestion farther away. Compare the same setup at a good time and a slow time.

  • Windows 10 and 11
  • 3 min read
  • Reviewed 27 November 2026

Build a small evening log

  1. Record one good period and the slowdown. Keep the device, connection type and test destination fixed. Note the clock time and the affected service.
  2. Check known household traffic. Pause a download or upload you control, then repeat. Activity on other devices can matter even when this PC is quiet.
  3. Repeat over Ethernet on another evening. If practical, compare a second device and more than one service. Avoid running several speed tests at once.

Look for a repeatable pattern

  • Only Wi-Fi gets worseCheck location, signal and nearby wireless activity. Keep the wired result as a comparison.
  • Pausing an upload helpsCompare delay with the upload active and paused. Queueing can affect responsiveness before the connection completely stops.
  • Several wired devices and services slow togetherA wider connection issue is a stronger possibility. Save repeated examples for your provider; time of day alone does not prove throttling.

Keep the same evidence

Use AdeeboMeter to note router and internet delay alongside this PC’s traffic. It cannot tell you every other device’s activity from this PC’s readings. Add the household activity you know about, then make a provider report during an incident.

Keep one useful comparison

Two or three comparable evenings tell a clearer story than unrelated speed-test screenshots. Include one good result, the slow result and the unchanged test setup.
