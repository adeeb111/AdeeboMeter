Fast speed test, but videos still buffer?

A speed test measures one transfer at one moment. Playback needs data to keep arriving. Compare what happens during the pause before buying a faster plan.

  • Windows 10 and 11
  • 3 min read
  • Reviewed 27 November 2026

Keep the video. Change one thing.

  1. Replay the same section. Keep the device and video quality fixed. Note whether the player shows a loading spinner or the picture simply stutters.
  2. Pause one known download or cloud upload. Watch whether playback improves. Note the change rather than assuming it caused the problem.
  3. Try the same video over Ethernet. If the problem remains, compare another video on the same service, then another service.

Follow the result

  • Ethernet helpsInvestigate the Wi-Fi link. Repeat near the router before changing equipment.
  • Only one service strugglesCompare its service status and player guidance. A speed test to another server tests a different path.
  • One device struggles everywhereCompare its CPU and disk activity. Smooth networking does not guarantee smooth video rendering.
Why a loading spinner matters

Rebuffering means the player is waiting for data. Dropped or uneven frames can have a different cause, including the device or player. The symptom helps choose the next comparison; it does not identify the cause on its own.

Run the check in AdeeboMeter

Watch router and internet response time while the video stalls. Use Streaming for its connection view, and the PC monitor when the picture stutters. Those readings support the comparison; they do not measure the player’s buffer.

Keep one useful comparison

Keep a before-and-after comparison: device, video quality, connection type, time and what changed. A faster plan is useful only when capacity is the limiting factor.
