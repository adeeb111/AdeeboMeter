Live football online - why your neighbour cheers first

Live streams are chunked, cached and buffered - 20 to 60 seconds behind the pitch.

A live match goes camera → encoder → packager (chunks) → CDN → your player's buffer. Each step adds seconds; classic HLS sits 20-45 s behind broadcast.

How the conversation goes

Stadium camerasEncoder

  1. Stadium cameras to Encoder: live picture

  2. Encoder → Packager: video in 2 s chunks

  3. Packager → CDN: chunk 4512

  4. Your app → CDN: newest chunk, please

  5. CDN → Your app: chunk 4512 - it plays about 30 s after the kick

When it goes wrong

Shrinking the buffer for lower delay makes stalls more likely. A goal alert on your phone beats the stream because notifications don't wait in a video buffer.

Going deeper

Low-latency HLS and DASH cut chunks into small parts and push them as soon as they exist, getting to 3-8 seconds - but with a smaller buffer, a Wi-Fi hiccup stalls the picture sooner. A big match is the hardest day for a CDN: millions ask for the same seconds at the same moment, so providers pre-position capacity and some use multicast-assisted delivery inside their own network. Satellite and IPTV multicast are usually closer to live than online apps. Picture stutter on a big match night is often the shared links, not your line.
