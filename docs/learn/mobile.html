Mobile, LTE and 5G

The mobile network in plain words

Your phone, the radio towers, and a core network that decides who you are and where your data goes.

The phone (UE) talks by radio to a base station (eNodeB in 4G, gNodeB in 5G). The base stations connect over fibre to the core, which checks your SIM, gives you an IP address and sends your data to the internet.

How the conversation goes

PhoneTower

  1. Phone to Tower: radio connection

  2. Tower → Core (control): who is this, may they connect?

  3. Core → Phone: welcome, here is your IP address

  4. Phone → Tower > Core (user plane): your data

  5. Core → Internet: your data, from the phone's address

When it goes wrong

Full bars and no internet usually means the data session (APN/DNN) failed, not the radio. Slow data with full bars is often a busy cell - many users sharing it.

Going deeper

The radio access network (RAN) handles the air: scheduling who transmits when, handovers between cells, signal quality. The core does everything else: authentication (from the SIM's secret key), mobility (which tower you're under), sessions (your IP address and the path for your data), policy and charging. Control (signalling) and user data travel separately: signalling to the core's control functions, your packets through user-plane gateways. "Registered but no data" is a classic case where the control side worked and the data session didn't. A cellular router or a phone hotspot looks like any other router to your PC - AdeeboMeter sees the link to it, DNS and reachability, not the radio or the core.
