The 4G core: MME, SGW, PGW, HSS

The MME signals; the gateways carry your data.

The MME authenticates you and tracks which area you're in. The SGW carries your data from the tower; the PGW gives you an IP address and connects to the internet (the APN). The HSS holds subscriber data.

How the conversation goes

eNodeBMME

  1. eNodeB to MME: initial UE message (attach request)

  2. MME → HSS: authentication info for this IMSI

  3. MME → SGW: create session

  4. SGW → PGW: create session (APN internet)

  5. SGW → MME: create session response - the PGW allocated IP 100.70.3.9

When it goes wrong

An MME problem means phones can't attach at all. A PGW or APN problem means attached but no data - the bars look fine.

Going deeper

Signalling goes eNodeB → MME (S1-MME, protocol S1AP with NAS inside). Data goes in GTP-U tunnels: eNodeB → SGW (S1-U) → PGW (S5/S8) → internet (SGi). Each tunnel has an ID (TEID), so a capture on S1-U shows your packets wrapped in GTP. The MME asks the HSS for your keys and profile (S6a, Diameter); the PCRF sets policy and the bearers' QoS. When you roam, the PGW can be your home network's - your traffic goes home first, which is why roaming can be slow.
