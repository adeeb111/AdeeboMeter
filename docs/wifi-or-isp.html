Is it your Wi-Fi or your internet provider?

When the connection slows down, try the same task on a second device. Then, if you can, connect your PC to the router by Ethernet and turn off Wi-Fi on that PC. Run the same check while the problem is happening.

Compare what happens

  • Ethernet works and Wi-Fi struggles. Focus on Wi-Fi. Try moving closer to the router and repeating the check.
  • Only one device struggles. Check that device for a busy download, a VPN, or high CPU usage. Try the same service on another device before blaming the connection.
  • Several devices struggle on Wi-Fi and Ethernet. Check whether several unrelated sites are affected. Look for an outage from your internet service provider (ISP). Your router, modem, or the connection beyond your home could still be involved.

Check the path from your PC

On Windows, run ipconfig in Command Prompt and look for Default Gateway under the active adapter. Ping that address to see how consistently your router answers. Compare it with an internet destination that normally answers ping. Some devices ignore ping, so treat a timeout as one clue and compare it with what happens in the app or browser.

If the router responds normally while several internet destinations slow down across devices, report the times and test results to your provider. If only one website or game is affected, check that service first.

Run a guided check with AdeeboMeter

Open Investigate in the Windows app and choose The internet is slow. Compare the PC, Wi-Fi, router, DNS, and internet results. If the issue repeats, monitor your internet speed over time or create a packet loss report for your ISP.
