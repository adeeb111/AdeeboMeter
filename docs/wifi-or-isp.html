Is it your Wi-Fi or your internet provider?

Run the same thing over a cable. If it's smooth on the cable and stutters on Wi-Fi, fix the wireless link before you call anyone.

Run the checks

  1. Write down the time and make the problem happen again with the same site, call or download.
  2. Try the same thing on a second device on the same Wi-Fi. On a phone, check it's on Wi-Fi, not mobile data.
  3. Plug the PC into the router with a cable, turn its Wi-Fi off, and repeat while the slowdown is happening.

Reading it

  • Smooth on the cableThe wireless link is the problem: distance, walls, or a crowded channel.
  • Only one device strugglesLook at that device: a download, an update or a VPN.
  • Slow on the cable tooLook at the router, then your provider's outage page.
  • Only one site strugglesTry another service. If it's fine, the problem is on that site's side.

An example

An evening video call freezes upstairs. On a cable the same laptop holds the call, and on Wi-Fi it improves next to the router. Start with coverage. If the call froze on the cable and on another device too, look at the router and the line instead.

Checking the router by hand

Run ipconfig and find the Default Gateway under the adapter you're using. Ping it once when things are fine and again during a slowdown, and compare with an internet address that usually answers. Routers sometimes answer pings last, so weigh this with the cable test.

Steady answers from your router while several devices struggle beyond it give support a better starting point. The modem, the provider and the wider path are still possible.

What to keep

One comparison from a good moment and one from the slowdown, with the connection type and the services that were affected.
